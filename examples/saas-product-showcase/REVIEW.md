# Review: Lumen, one product in two art directions (fourth cut)

Answered on 2026-11-19 from the rendered files: a sheet at 1.3 frames a second of each, and the motion strips
with their numbers. Sound was measured, not heard. Lumen is an example product; its interface, mark and sound
effects are placeholders, and there is no narration or music.

The last review's main finding was that the two cuts were one composition in two colours. They now differ in
the things an art direction is made of:

|                        | `video.json`, editorial luxury                                     | `video-dark.json`, cinematic product                                      |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Length                 | 15.1 s, one take                                                   | 15.4 s, one take                                                          |
| HyperFrames check      | 0 errors, 0 warnings                                               | 0 errors, 0 warnings                                                      |
| Composition system     | A page recomposed: headline and product trade places               | The product is always the centre, an object in a dark room                |
| How the product enters | Small, low in the corner under a very large headline               | Through one element: a lit input in the dark; the room fills on the click |

## Remaining weaknesses

1. The product is an example. A real interface would do more for "premium" than any change here.
2. No music and no voice; the effects are synthesised.
3. Editorial: a third of its frames are still, and its share section is the weakest composition.
4. Cinematic: the change from dark to the white interface is progressive now, but its first frames are still abrupt.
5. Cropping and text size are not checked automatically; only contrast and motion are.
6. Easing and rhythm at full speed were not judged; that needs a person watching the files.
